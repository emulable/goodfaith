# Bodies First

Weight rests on bodies. A nation has no stomach, a budget cannot miss a meal, and a company has never lost a night's sleep, so whenever an abstraction appears to suffer, keep going downward until the people inside it become visible. One question runs everything here. Did weight come off a body, and did another body catch it?

A body is anyone who can go hungry, get tired, be frightened, or be injured. Weight is a load no decision of theirs can lift. Hunger cannot be elected away, and neither can cold, exhaustion, fear, or a body that stopped working right. How the load arrived changes nothing about the reading, which keeps every conversation out of the long argument about whether somebody brought it on themselves.

## Moved is not off

Weight taken off one back and set on another has not come off. Weight scheduled for later has not come off. Weight pressed under a lid until it splits somewhere quieter has not come off.

A park empties while every person who slept there stays unsheltered. A family solves elder care by putting nearly every difficult task on one daughter. A hospital shortens stays by pushing unpaid care onto relatives. An agency shortens a waiting list by narrowing eligibility. A government lowers this year's costs by leaving deferred maintenance to whoever pays for it later. Each of those looks like improvement from the right distance, and in each of them the burden moved.

Transfer hides best when the receiving person is quiet, far away, poorly measured, politically weak, outside the institution, or not yet born. Weight collects in exactly those places, because the people keeping records are usually somewhere else. So the inspection runs two questions rather than one. What became lighter, and who became heavier?

Weight can genuinely leave, which is the outcome to aim for. Grass crossed with trodden dirt paths represents thousands of small daily frictions, and paving the routes people already walk removes them. Nobody inherits the walking. The grass recovers. Nothing was transferred, because the design changed so the force stops arriving.

## A referral is not treatment

Naming the result and naming the method are separate jobs, and mixing them produces easy victories that deliver nothing. The application was submitted. The referral was issued. The money was allocated. The beds were funded. The training was completed. Every one of those may describe real work, and not one tells anybody whether a person got lighter.

Leave the endpoint vague and substitutions walk in unchallenged. A referral starts to look like treatment when treatment was never defined. A shelter allocation starts to look like housing when housing was never defined. A fine starts to look like prevention when prevention was never defined.

So name the endpoint in ordinary words first. A person with no food needs food in the mouth. A person without medicine needs the medicine in hand. A parent losing three days of wages to register a birth needs the process to stop taking three days. Then let every method compete to get there, and check the result at the place where relief was supposed to arrive rather than at the desk that sent it.

## Recurring human effort means a structure should be carrying the load

Attention, patience, memory, and vigilance are finite supplies inside real skulls, and a system drawing on them every day imposes a continuing burden even when each demand looks small.

A dangerous edge protected by a warning sign asks every person to notice the danger every time. A barrier does that work continuously and asks nobody. A form requiring applicants to understand an obscure bureaucracy makes each applicant carry knowledge the institution already has. A better form carries it instead. A painted line asks every driver to hold a line on the worst day of their year, and a concrete curb holds it for them.

Watchfulness is itself a load, which is the part that hides. Every driver on that painted road pays attention on every trip, forever, whether or not anyone would have drifted. A necessary watch and an expensive watch are the same watch. Fraud screening catches fraud and taxes every honest applicant's afternoon. A manager who checks everything catches mistakes and spends their staff's nerve. Before asking how to staff a watch better, ask what it would take to abolish whatever requires watching.

## Predictable behavior is a design condition

People cut across grass when the paved path makes the trip too long. People hide bad news when reporting it reliably brings punishment. People delay care when treatment costs too much money, time, transport, and paperwork. People stop applying for help when the application costs more time and humiliation than they can spare. Workers take shortcuts when the official procedure cannot be finished in the time allowed.

Repeated predictable behavior is information about the surrounding design. Discipline sometimes changes behavior, and redesign sometimes makes the harmful interaction unnecessary. The second result keeps working when nobody is especially patient, obedient, brave, rested, or attentive.

Pride belongs on that list. Call somebody a monster and you have handed them a load heavier than the one under discussion, because they now have to defend their standing as a human being, and that defense consumes everything they had available for looking at whoever is underneath. Naming what a person is doing raises the cost of continuing, and conduct comes with an exit attached. Naming what a person is offers no exit at all, which is why it welds the weight in place. Build the exit with the same care used to build the pressure, and tie a reversal to a changed reading rather than to a confession.

## Counting works when sympathy runs out

Sympathy varies. People feel strongly for some strangers and almost nothing for others, and allegiance, familiarity, and fear all change what a person notices. Any procedure depending on a steady supply of warm feeling fails the moment the feeling runs out.

Hours in a queue stay hours whether or not the observer likes the person waiting. The distance to a clinic stays the same distance. An empty stomach stays empty when nobody finds the hungry person admirable. Somebody with very little sympathy can still count the hours, inspect the route, find the failed handoff, and repair the process, which means nobody has to become a better person before somebody else can become lighter.

The same property makes this workable across disagreement. One person says a policy is just and another says it is an outrage, both sincerely, and the argument can run for a generation without either side able to check anything. Two people who disagree about nearly everything can still produce the same count of trips to an office and meals missed last month. Shared measurement stays available where shared conclusions are not.

## Say the obvious repair out loud

Water should reach the household without a four-hour walk. Registering a birth should not cost a parent three days of wages. A sick person should reach treatment before the illness becomes an emergency. A dangerous machine should stop before it reaches a worker.

Somebody will call that unrealistic, and the word gives the work nothing. A real constraint can be described closely enough for another person to go inspect it. A pipe may need money nobody allocated. A clinic may need twelve nurses when three can be hired this year. A road may cross unstable ground. Retreat only as far as the constraint requires, and leave the missing distance visible afterward.

The person saying unrealistic often knows exactly where the wall stands, because they have walked into it. Asking them to describe it turns an objection into expertise. *What stops it?* invites somebody to contribute what they know, and *why are you against helping?* invites them to defend themselves, which consumes the afternoon and produces nothing.

## Aim for boring

Successful safety work goes invisible. A railing stays in place. A truck collects the rubbish on schedule. A form takes one visit instead of three. A machine stops before a worker reaches the dangerous part. A pipe brings water to the building. Nobody has to be heroic for any of that, and nobody has to remember the original argument every morning, because the argument became infrastructure.

Some of this gets called radical, and the word costs nothing to ignore. Engineering toward the people carrying the weight is no more radical than a lap-and-shoulder belt, than mine safety rules written after enough miners suffocated, than a truck that comes on Thursdays. Each was strange once, and each is now so ordinary that nobody remembers arguing about it.

Somebody has to be the first person in a room to say the unremarkable sentence. They spend a decade being called dangerous, and then it turns into furniture.

The purpose is fewer people needing rescue, endurance, courage, patience, or public sympathy to get through an ordinary week. May we live in boring times. They will not actually be boring, because a person with no load has range, and range gets spent on mornings nobody thinks to record.
